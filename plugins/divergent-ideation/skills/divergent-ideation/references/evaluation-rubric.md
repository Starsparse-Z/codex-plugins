# Idea Evaluation Rubric

Use this only after generation and clustering are complete.

## Hard Gates

Reject or quarantine an idea when:

- usefulness is `0`: it does not help the stated beneficiary or outcome;
- feasibility is `0`: no credible path exists with available time,
  technology, permissions, or capital;
- it depends on an illegal, deceptive, or materially harmful mechanism;
- its claimed advantage depends entirely on an unverifiable fact.

An infeasible idea may still remain in the `Wild` portfolio for learning, but
it should not be presented as an implementation recommendation.

## Score Dimensions

Use `0-3` for each dimension.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Usefulness | No outcome impact | Marginal | Clear benefit | Decisive benefit |
| Novelty | Existing standard | Minor variation | Different mechanism | Category-changing |
| Feasibility | Not possible now | Major blockers | Credible path | Ready to test |
| Evidence | Pure assertion | Analogy or weak signal | Direct but limited evidence | Strong tested evidence |
| Reversibility | Irreversible | Expensive reversal | Reversible with effort | Cheap experiment |
| Strategic fit | Conflicts with goals | Weak fit | Supports goals | Strengthens a core advantage |
| Risk | Severe unmanaged risk | Serious unknowns | Manageable | Robust and contained |

Score usefulness, novelty, and feasibility for every option. Add the remaining
dimensions only when they can change the decision.

Do not hide uncertainty inside an average. Show the dimension scores or a
short reason for each selection.

## Portfolio Construction

Choose across roles, not just by total score:

- `Safe`: highest usefulness and feasibility;
- `Asymmetric`: credible upside with limited downside and a cheap test;
- `Wild`: high novelty that challenges the current framing.

When attention is limited, recommend no more than three options to test and no
more than five for shortlist review.

## First Test

For every selected option define:

- the weakest assumption;
- the cheapest test that could falsify it;
- the evidence threshold for continuing;
- the timebox, preferably 24-72 hours;
- what would make the team stop, narrow, or pivot.

Prefer behavioral evidence over opinions and direct tests over elaborate
research when a safe small experiment is possible.

## Common Biases

- **Fluency-originality confound**: people who generate more ideas have more
  chances to produce an original one. Compare the top few ideas, not only raw
  totals.
- **Anchoring**: the first idea shapes the scoring scale. Randomize or blind
  the order when possible.
- **Feasibility bias**: familiar ideas look easier. Evaluate novelty before
  assigning implementation effort.
- **Novelty theater**: novelty without usefulness is entertainment, not a
  strategy.
- **Averaging away the winner**: a high-variance idea can be more valuable than
  a consistently average one.
- **False precision**: a numeric score does not turn assumptions into facts.

## Decision Language

State one of:

- `Commit`: evidence is sufficient for the next bounded step;
- `Test`: the direction is promising but an assumption remains unproven;
- `Park`: keep as a deliberate option and state the trigger for revisiting;
- `Reject`: a hard gate or repeated evidence failure rules it out.

Do not use "maybe" without naming the missing evidence or next test.
