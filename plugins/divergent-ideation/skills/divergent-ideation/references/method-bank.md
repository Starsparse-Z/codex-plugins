# Ideation Method Bank

Use the smallest combination that creates genuine mechanism diversity. Do not
run every method mechanically.

## Method Selection

| Situation | Strong starting methods |
|---|---|
| Vague problem | How Might We, perspective rotation, analogous domains |
| Existing product | SCAMPER, subtraction, task unification |
| Stuck in one direction | Random entry, reversal, dominant idea escape |
| Complex contradiction | TRIZ contradiction, ideal final result, trimming |
| Need many possibilities | Crazy 8s, morphological analysis, forced combinations |
| Need category-breaking ideas | Synectics, cross-domain collision, fantasy analogy |
| Need strategic options | Six Hats, scenario reversal, stakeholder rotation |

## How Might We

Use to widen the problem before proposing solutions.

Prompt pattern:

```text
How might we <outcome> for <beneficiary> without assuming <current solution>?
```

Create three variants: broad, constraint-focused, and disruptive. Reject any
question that already contains a preferred solution.

## SCAMPER

Apply one lens at a time:

- Substitute a mechanism, actor, input, or channel;
- Combine two jobs, products, teams, or business models;
- Adapt a solution from another domain;
- Modify or magnify one important variable;
- Put an existing capability to a different use;
- Eliminate the most expensive or complicated component;
- Reverse the workflow, incentives, or roles.

For each lens, capture the changed mechanism, not just the changed feature.

## Crazy 8s

Generate eight rough ideas in a short timebox. Do not elaborate. Use when
self-censorship or over-polishing is slowing the work. Follow with a diversity
audit because speed often produces variations of one dominant idea.

## Synectics

Use analogies to create conceptual distance.

- Direct analogy: find a structurally similar system in another domain;
- Personal analogy: become the user, system, or obstacle;
- Symbolic analogy: compress the conflict into a paradox or oxymoron;
- Fantasy analogy: imagine the ideal solution without current constraints;
- Excursion: leave the problem, explore an unrelated domain, then force-fit
  the discoveries back.

Always state the structural mapping. A decorative metaphor is not an analogy.

## Lateral Thinking

- Random entry: introduce an unrelated concept and force a connection;
- Provocation: state an intentionally unreasonable "what if";
- Dominant idea escape: identify the assumption everyone shares, then negate
  it;
- Concept fan: move up to a broader purpose, then descend through different
  ways to achieve it;
- Challenge: ask why the current boundary, sequence, or rule exists.

Generate first, then test whether the movement produced a usable mechanism.

## Systematic Inventive Thinking

- Subtraction: remove a component considered essential;
- Multiplication: duplicate a component and make the copies different;
- Division: split a product, process, or task and rearrange the parts;
- Task unification: assign an existing task to another component;
- Variable dependency: connect two previously independent variables.

These methods work best when the solution already has a defined set of
components.

## TRIZ

Use for technical or business contradictions.

- State the improving variable and the worsening variable;
- Describe the contradiction without proposing a compromise;
- Seek separation in time, space, condition, or scale;
- Define the ideal final result;
- Use trimming: remove a component while preserving its useful function;
- Search other domains for a principle that resolves the same contradiction.

Do not force a 40-principles catalog. Use it only when a real contradiction is
visible.

## Morphological Analysis

Build a matrix of independent dimensions such as user, channel, pricing,
technology, delivery, and trust mechanism. Select combinations that are
currently absent. Discard combinations that violate hard constraints before
they enter the option set.

## Six Hats

Rotate through the perspectives separately:

- facts and missing information;
- benefits and optimism;
- risks and failure modes;
- creative alternatives;
- emotional and stakeholder response;
- process control and next decision.

Use after divergent generation when the group needs a disciplined review.
Do not let the risk hat dominate before the creative hat has produced options.

## Perspective Rotation

Regenerate the problem from the viewpoint of:

- a skeptical buyer;
- a non-user or resister;
- an operator responsible for maintenance;
- a regulator or safety reviewer;
- a competitor;
- a future user ten years from now;
- the system itself.

Record which assumption changed under each perspective.
